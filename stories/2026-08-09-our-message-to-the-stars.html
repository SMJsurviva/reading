Our Message to the Stars

2026-08-09 · Something Different · a true story · 3-minute read

Imagine sending a letter that might travel for millions of years before someone finds it. That's what Carl Sagan and his team did in 1977. They put together a special record, called the Voyager Golden Record, and sent it on a journey into deep space aboard two spacecraft, Voyager 1 and Voyager 2.

This gold-plated copper disk is like a time capsule from Earth. It's meant for any intelligent life forms in space who might discover it. The record holds a story of our planet and its people. It has natural sounds like wind, thunder, and even the songs of whales. It also includes human sounds, such as footsteps, laughter (Carl Sagan's!), and even a kiss.

To represent humanity, the team gathered greetings in 55 different languages. You can hear a greeting in Korean, too! There’s also music from many cultures and times, with composers like Bach and Mozart, as well as artists like Louis Armstrong and Chuck Berry. The record even holds an hour-long recording of Ann Druyan’s brainwaves, thinking about Earth and falling in love, compressed into one minute of audio.

The record contains 116 images showing life on Earth. These pictures explain who we are, where we live, and what we look like. They show animals, plants, landscapes, and people from different cultures in their daily lives. There are even diagrams of DNA and human anatomy. The team wanted to show a broad picture of our world.

Carl Sagan said that launching this “bottle” into the “cosmic ocean” says something hopeful about life on Earth. These two spacecraft, carrying our message, are now very far from home, beyond our solar system. Voyager 1 is the farthest human-made object from Earth, still sending back data even today, almost 50 years later.
