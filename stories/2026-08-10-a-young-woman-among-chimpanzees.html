A Young Woman Among Chimpanzees

2026-08-10 · A Famous Life · a true story · 3-minute read

From a young age, Jane Goodall loved animals and dreamed of Africa. In 1957, she finally made it to Kenya, working as a secretary. One day, she called a famous scientist, Louis Leakey, hoping to talk about animals. He was looking for someone to study chimpanzees in the wild, though he didn't tell her this right away. Instead, he hired her as his secretary.

After working for him for a while, Leakey sent Jane to London to learn about primates. Then, in 1960, he helped her go to Gombe Stream National Park in Tanzania. Jane was only 26 years old and had no university degree. It was unusual for a woman to work in this field, but her mother went with her, which helped convince the park warden it was safe.

For five years, Jane lived among the chimpanzees. She gave them names like David Greybeard and Flo, instead of numbers, which was very different from what other scientists did. She watched them closely and learned amazing things. She saw chimpanzees use tools, like stripping leaves from twigs to catch termites. This surprised everyone, as people thought only humans made and used tools.

Jane also saw their personalities, their strong family bonds, and their emotions like joy and sorrow. She even observed them hugging and kissing. But she also saw a darker side: chimpanzees could be aggressive and violent. Her discoveries showed that chimpanzees shared many traits with humans, changing how people viewed the animal kingdom. Her work was so important that Louis Leakey arranged for her to study for a PhD at the University of Cambridge, even though she didn't have a bachelor's degree.
