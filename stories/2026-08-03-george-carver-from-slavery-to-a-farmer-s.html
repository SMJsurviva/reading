George Carver: From Slavery to a Farmer's Friend

2026-08-03 · A Famous Life · a true story · 3-minute read

Imagine being born into a time when people were owned by others. That was George Washington Carver's beginning. He was born into slavery in Missouri sometime in the early 1860s. His birthdate was a mystery, lost to the time before slavery ended in his state.

His early life was full of hardship. Raiders kidnapped him and his mother when he was just a baby, but thankfully, a kind man named John Bentley found him. After slavery was abolished, Moses and Susan Carver, who had once owned George, raised him and his brother as their own. They encouraged George's curiosity and helped him learn to read and write.

George loved plants. He saw how hard life was for poor farmers, especially those who only grew cotton. Cotton took a lot out of the soil, leaving it tired and unable to grow good food. George wanted to help.

He became a scientist who studied plants. He discovered that planting different crops, like peanuts and sweet potatoes, could help the soil recover. These plants also gave farmers other food to eat and things to sell.

George worked at a school called Tuskegee Institute. He didn't just teach in a classroom. He created a special truck, like a traveling classroom, that went out to the farms. He wanted to share his knowledge directly with the farmers who needed it most.

He wrote many helpful guides, called bulletins, for farmers. These guides explained how to prevent the soil from getting tired, how to grow bigger crops, and how to keep food from spoiling. Many farmers wrote to him, asking for advice. They trusted George Carver, the man who understood their struggles and had brilliant ideas about the earth.

George Carver's discoveries helped not only farmers but also the environment. He was a true inventor and a great mind. Even though he was born into slavery, he became a famous scientist, admired by many, and a source of hope for countless farmers.
