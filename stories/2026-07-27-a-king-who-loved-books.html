A King Who Loved Books

2026-07-27 · A Famous Life · a true story · 3-minute read

King Sejong, born Yi To, was the third son of King Taejong. Nobody expected him to become king. This meant fewer records were kept about his early life, but one story gives us a peek into the kind of person he was.

Young Yi To loved to read so much that his father, King Taejong, worried about his health. King Taejong even took away his son's books. But Yi To was clever. He managed to hide one book and read it again and again, many times over. Historians aren't sure if this story is completely true, but they do say it fits with Yi To’s lifelong love for learning.

Later, his father decided to make Yi To the crown prince, not his older brother. King Taejong saw that Yi To was intelligent and loved to study. Soon after, Yi To became King Sejong at the age of 21.

King Sejong’s reign was a time of great learning and new ideas. He created the Hall of Worthies, a special government group that was like a research center. They worked on many important projects, like the first Korean calendar and new medical and farming books. He also made sure that scholars had time to study on their own, even giving them money to do research.

But the most famous thing King Sejong did was create Hangul, the Korean alphabet. Before Hangul, most ordinary people could not read or write because the Chinese characters used were very difficult. King Sejong understood that everyone deserved to read and write. He created Hangul so that more people could easily learn and share knowledge. He truly believed in helping his people learn and grow.
