The Amazing Octopus: A Creature of Blue Blood and Clever Escapes

2026-07-25 · The Natural World · a true story · 3-minute read

Imagine a creature with three hearts and blue blood. That’s the octopus! These amazing animals live in oceans all over the world, from shallow coral reefs to the very deep seabed. They come in many sizes. The smallest, Octopus wolfi, is only about one inch long. But the giant Pacific octopus can weigh more than 150 pounds and have arms almost 16 feet long.

Octopuses are soft-bodied, eight-armed animals. Their mouths have a sharp beak, like a bird's, hidden among their arms. Each arm has suckers that help them hold onto things or move around. These suckers can even taste what they touch!

One of the most special things about an octopus is its blood. It’s blue! This is because their blood uses a copper-rich protein to carry oxygen. They also have three hearts: one big heart pumps blood around the body, and two smaller hearts pump it through their gills. This unique system means they get tired quickly when swimming, so they often prefer to crawl.

Octopuses are incredibly smart and good at problem-solving. They can change their shape, allowing them to squeeze through tiny spaces. Even a big octopus can get through a hole as small as one inch wide. This makes them masters of escape. They also have many ways to protect themselves from danger. They can squirt out a cloud of ink, change their skin color to blend in with their surroundings, or quickly jet away through the water.

Their eyes are very complex, similar to human eyes. They also have a complex nervous system, with two-thirds of their brain power located in their arms! This means their arms can act almost on their own. Octopuses are truly fascinating creatures, full of surprises.
