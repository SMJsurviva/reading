The Many Faces of Cinderella

2026-08-05 · Stories of the World · a true story · 3-minute read

Many people know the story of Cinderella. A young girl lives a hard life but then finds great fortune and marries a prince. This tale is told all over the world. It has thousands of different versions. One of the oldest known stories is from ancient Greece. It tells of a slave girl named Rhodopis. An eagle took her sandal and dropped it into the king of Egypt’s lap. The king searched for the sandal's owner and married her.

Over time, many writers shared their own versions. In 1634, an Italian writer named Giambattista Basile wrote “La Gatta Cenerentola.” Later, in 1697, Charles Perrault's French version, “Cendrillon,” became very popular. This is the story most English speakers know. Then, in 1812, the Brothers Grimm published their German version, “Aschenputtel.”

But the story of a mistreated girl finding love and success isn't just European. In China, there's the tale of Ye Xian, who loses a golden shoe. In Vietnam, Tấm, a kind girl, also uses magical fish bones to help her. Korea has its own version called Kongjwi and Patjwi. Kongjwi, like other Cinderellas, is abused by her stepmother and stepsister. She gets help from animals and a fairy. She loses her shoe, and a magistrate finds her and marries her.

What makes Kongjwi's story different is that her troubles continue even after she marries. Her stepsister, Patjwi, tries to hurt her again. But Kongjwi is strong and gets her revenge. She shows that a Cinderella story can be different in each place, reflecting the culture where it is told. These stories remind us that good can come from difficult times, and that strength can be found even in the most unfortunate circumstances.
