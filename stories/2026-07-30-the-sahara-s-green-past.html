The Sahara's Green Past

2026-07-30 · Places · a true story · 3-minute read

Imagine a vast desert, a place so dry that rain is a rare visitor. This is the Sahara, a huge land in North Africa. Today, it’s known for its sand dunes and scorching heat. But this wasn't always the case. For thousands of years, this area has changed, like a slow breath. Sometimes it was a dry desert, and other times, it was covered in green grass, like a wide savanna.

Scientists believe this change happens in a long cycle, about every 20,000 years. This cycle is caused by how the Earth turns and moves around the sun. It affects where the rain falls in North Africa. So, the Sahara we know today was once a very different place. It was a land with lakes, where animals like hippos might have lived, and plants grew abundantly.

This giant desert is not just sand. It has rocky hills, plains covered in gravel, and dry riverbeds that fill up only after a rare rain. Some sand dunes are taller than very tall buildings! Wind and the scarce rain have shaped this amazing landscape over time. There are even mountains in the middle of the desert, some of them formed by volcanoes. The highest point is a mountain in Chad, which is actually a volcano.

Even in the very dry middle parts of the Sahara, there are smaller areas where plants can survive. Along the edges, near mountains or where water might collect, you can find grass and even trees. It’s hard to imagine, but this vast, dry land has a history of being much greener and full of life. This changing story of the Sahara reminds us that even the most extreme places on Earth have a past that might surprise us.
