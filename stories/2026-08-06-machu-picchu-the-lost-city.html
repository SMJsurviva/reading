Machu Picchu: The Lost City

2026-08-06 · Places · a true story · 3-minute read

Imagine a city hidden high in the mountains of Peru. It was built by the Inca people in the 15th century, around 1450. This city, now called Machu Picchu, sat on a narrow ridge, surrounded by deep canyons and green mountains. It was a beautiful place, with finely built stone walls and important buildings like the Temple of the Sun.

Historians believe Machu Picchu was a special retreat for the Inca emperor, Pachacuti. About 750 people lived there, most of them working to support the emperor and his family. Many of these workers were not from Machu Picchu itself; they came from different parts of Peru. They even brought llamas and alpacas from higher mountains to Machu Picchu for food and their soft fur. The city also had guinea pigs, which were used in special ceremonies.

To grow food, the Inca built amazing stone terraces on the mountainside. These terraces helped the water drain away and kept the soil rich. They grew corn and potatoes, though not enough for everyone. Food had to be brought in from nearby valleys.

Machu Picchu was used for about 80 years. Then, around the mid-16th century, it was abandoned. This was about the time the Spanish were conquering other parts of the Inca Empire. It’s even possible that many people in Machu Picchu died from diseases brought by Europeans before the Spanish soldiers ever reached the area. The jungle slowly grew over the city, hiding it from the outside world.

For centuries, few people knew about Machu Picchu. Locals sometimes called it “Huayna Picchu,” which means “young peak” in their language. But the Spanish never found it. It remained a secret city, lost to most of the world, until a Peruvian explorer named Agustín Lizárraga visited it in 1902. Then, in 1911, an American historian named Hiram Bingham brought Machu Picchu to international attention, revealing this magnificent hidden city to the world.
