A Father's Promise: Anne Frank's Diary

2026-07-31 · Family Stories · a true story · 3-minute read

After World War II ended, Otto Frank, Anne’s father, returned to Amsterdam. He was the only one in his family who survived the Holocaust. He found that his secretaries, Miep Gies and Bep Voskuijl, had saved Anne’s diary. Anne had always wanted to be an author, and her father remembered her wishes.

Deeply moved by his daughter's dream, Otto Frank decided to publish her diary. It was first printed in Dutch in 1947. Then, in 1952, it was translated into English and called *The Diary of a Young Girl*. Since then, it has been translated into more than 70 languages. Through this book, Anne became one of the most talked-about Jewish victims of the Holocaust. Her diary became one of the world's best-known books and has been used for many plays and films.

Anne was born in Germany in 1929. When she was four, her family moved to Amsterdam because of Adolf Hitler. They were Jewish, and times became very dangerous for them. In 1942, the family went into hiding in a secret room behind a bookcase in Otto’s workplace. Anne started writing in her diary, which she named “Kitty,” on her thirteenth birthday that year. She wrote about her daily life, her feelings, and her dreams. She wanted to be a journalist and said, “I want to go on living even after my death!”

Two years later, in 1944, the family was arrested and sent to concentration camps. Anne and her sister Margot died there a few months later, likely from an illness. Otto Frank lived to make sure Anne’s wish came true. By publishing her diary, he made sure Anne would live on, just as she had hoped. Her words still touch people all over the world today.
